What counts as a valid reason?

"My assessment went up too much" is not, on its own, a valid ground for a complaint. What matters is whether the assessed value reflects actual market value as of July 1 — not how much it changed from last year.

How the appeal process works, briefly

This isn't a legal filing service — it's a plain-language guide plus a referral. The actual process (called PARP) is free to file and runs every year on a tight, fixed timeline.

1. File a complaint

Free, filed directly with BC Assessment, by the January deadline.

2. PARP hearing

A short phone hearing, typically 30 minutes, held between February and mid-March.

3. Decision

Usually given verbally at the hearing, with a written notice mailed by April 7.

4. Escalate if needed

Not satisfied? Appeal to the Property Assessment Appeal Board by April 30 — firm, no extensions.

Why BC property assessments go up, and who this applies to

Whether you're in Vancouver, Surrey, Burnaby, Richmond, Kelowna, Victoria, or a smaller municipality anywhere in BC, the same provincial process applies: BC Assessment values every property as of July 1 the year before your notice arrives, using recent comparable sales in your specific neighbourhood. A big jump usually reflects a genuinely hot local market, a nearby sale that came in high, or a recorded renovation, none of which are grounds for a complaint on their own. What matters is whether the resulting number still lines up with what similar homes near you actually sold for around that valuation date. That's exactly what the free check above estimates, and it's the same question a PARP panel will ask if you file a complaint.

Common questions

Is my assessment worth appealing?

It depends on whether your assessed value is actually higher than what comparable properties sold for around the valuation date, not simply on how much it increased from last year. Our free check above gives a general read, but a professional review of your specific comparables is the most reliable way to know.

Is it free to file a complaint?

Yes, filing with the Property Assessment Review Panel (the first level) is free. If you need to escalate to the Property Assessment Appeal Board, there's a $30 fee for residential properties.

Do I need a lawyer?

No, a PARP hearing is designed for homeowners to represent themselves. Some people choose to work with a property tax professional, which is what our referral connects you with, but it isn't required.
